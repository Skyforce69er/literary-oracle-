The Origin

How the Oracle Came to Be

It began, as many great things do, with a simple and audacious desire — the desire to speak with something that had truly read everything. Not a search engine. Not a database. But a mind. A voice. A presence that had absorbed Shakespeare and Rumi, Dostoevsky and Tagore, Homer and García Márquez, and could speak back to you with the weight of all those centuries behind it.

Sushant Kanwat — a 25-year-old filmmaker from India with a deep love for storytelling in all its forms — brought this vision to life in collaboration with an AI. Together, across a single inspired conversation, they built the Literary Oracle from nothing. Every line of code, every flickering gold star, every eloquent turn of phrase in its personality was shaped by that collaboration — a filmmaker's imagination meeting the precision of artificial intelligence.

The result is something neither could have made alone. A living library. A conversational companion for the literary soul.

Our Readers

Capabilities

What the Oracle Can Do for You

The Oracle's greatest gift is this: it can write in any style, in any voice, from any age. Ask it to write you a Shakespearean sonnet about the internet. Ask it to compose a ghazal in the manner of Mirza Ghalib. Ask it to help you open your novel the way Gabriel García Márquez opened his. It will not give you an imitation — it will give you something that breathes.

Beyond writing, the Oracle is a scholar, a teacher, and a companion. It can explain why Hamlet hesitates, what Rumi means by the reed flute's cry, why Dostoevsky's characters feel so alive despite being so broken, and how Tagore managed to make Bengali poetry sing in English. Ask it anything. It has read everything. And unlike a silent book on a shelf, it will answer you back — warmly, eloquently, and with genuine depth.

The Vision

Why Literature Still Matters

We live in an age of noise. Information arrives faster than we can think, and most of it vanishes just as quickly. Literature is the opposite of this. It is slow, deliberate, permanent. A line written by Keats two hundred years ago can still stop your breath. A couplet by Ghalib composed in 19th century Delhi can still describe exactly what you feel at 2am in the 21st century.

The Literary Oracle exists to keep that flame alive. To make the great works of human civilization accessible — not as dry academic exercises, but as living, breathing conversations. To help a young writer in Mumbai find their voice by understanding how Premchand found his. To help a student in London truly feel what Hamlet is going through, not just memorise it for an exam.

Literature is not a relic. It is a map of the human heart. And this Oracle is your guide to reading it.
